Product Requirements Document

Contextual Video Coaching

Training that reaches the FSE inside the job, at the moment it is useful — and proves it landed.

ProductGG App and Force App — Field Sales Executive mobile applications
OwnerProduct
StatusDraft for review

ReviewersDesign, Frontend, Backend, Field Ops, L&D

1. Why we are building this

An FSE learns the job in a classroom on day three and is then expected to remember it in front of a merchant six weeks later. Everything since has been a PDF on a group chat.

The cost shows up in specific, measurable places:

  • QRs mapped to the wrong MID, so a merchant's money settles into someone else's account
  • SoundBox rentals sold without the UPI Autopay mandate, so the rental never collects
  • Re-KYC left half-finished, so the merchant's settlements freeze
  • Loan pitches made without the disclosures we are obliged to give

These are not knowledge gaps in general. They are four or five specific moments, on four or five specific screens, where the FSE needed thirty seconds of guidance and did not have it.

The bet. Training delivered on the screen where the work happens, at the moment it is needed, in ninety seconds, will change behaviour where classroom training and PDFs have not. And where the content is non-negotiable, we can require proof it was understood.

2. Who this is for

WhoWhat they need
The FSEHelp at the moment of the task, without losing their place. They are standing outside a shop, on a phone, on patchy data, with a merchant waiting.
Their managerTo know who has been trained on what, and to have something to point at in a gate meeting.
L&D and OpsTo publish a training in an afternoon, aim it at the right people, and see whether it worked — without an app release.

3. How we will know it worked

MeasureWhat it tells us
Error rate on the taught behaviourThe real measure. Mis-mapped QRs, missing Autopay mandates, stalled Re-KYC — before and after, for FSEs who completed versus those who have not.
Completion rate of mandatory contentWhether the enforcement mechanism works at all.
First-attempt pass rateBelow 60%, the content is not teaching what the assessment tests. Above 85%, the questions are too easy to be worth asking.
Dismissal rate per placementWhether a nudge is help or noise. A high rate on one screen is a placement problem, not a content problem.
Drop-off point within a videoWhere attention is lost. If most FSEs stop at forty seconds, the video should be forty seconds.

4. The shape of the thing

Three objects, and the separation between them is the whole design:

IsAnswers
ContentA video, audio clip, PDF or image — with its language versionsWhat are we showing?
TrainingAn ordered set of content, the widget it appears in, and its assessmentWhat should they learn, and how do we know they did?
PlacementA training put on a screen, in a position, for an audience, under rulesWho sees it, where, how hard does it push?

Why separate training from placement. The same QR-mapping training belongs on the home screen for everyone, and on the merchant screen for FSEs missing their target, with different urgency in each case. One object, placed twice. Without the split we maintain two copies of the same content and they drift apart.

5. User flows

5.1 The everyday case — help offered, not forced

Ajay opens a merchant's SoundBox plan screen. A mini banner sits below the plan selector: “How to explain the ₹125 plan · 0:40”.

  1. He taps it. The banner expands in place — the plan selector stays on screen and reachable.
  2. He taps play. The video opens over the screen, in Hindi, because Hindi is one of the languages uploaded for it.
  3. A merchant interrupts him at 0:18. He closes it.
  4. Ten minutes later he taps the banner again. It opens at 0:18, with a Start over beside it.
  5. It ends. There is no assessment on this one, so it is marked complete and the banner shows a tick.
  6. He has now seen it twice; the placement is capped at three views, so it will appear once more and then stop.

5.2 The enforced case — a mandatory SOP

The QR mapping SOP changes. Ops publishes it as an interstitial on the home screen, mandatory.

  1. Ajay opens the app. The home screen is sealed: a gate listing the training and how many steps remain. There is no dismiss and no “later”.
  2. The only action is Complete now, which takes him to My trainings.
  3. He opens the training. Skipping is off — mandatory content cannot be skimmed — so the scrubber will not go past what he has actually watched.
  4. The video ends. The assessment unlocks: three questions drawn to a paper worth 3, needing 2 to pass.
  5. He answers. Nothing tells him right or wrong as he goes. He can change an answer until he moves on.
  6. He scores 1. Assessment failed. The video is marked unwatched again and the assessment re-locks — he must watch it before retrying. The message explains why this matters: money settles to the wrong account.
  7. Second time he passes. The gate lifts. The card moves to Completed and offers a certificate.

5.3 The long case — onboarding in My trainings

A new joiner is assigned New joiner essentials: four videos and one assessment at the end, as a My trainings page training.

  1. It appears in My trainings under Pending, sorted near the top because it is mandatory.
  2. Opening it shows the four videos in order. The first is ringed and marked NEXT; a sticky button at the bottom says Start.
  3. The assessment sits at the end, visibly locked: “Finish the remaining 4 videos to unlock this.”
  4. He does two, closes the app, comes back. The card reads 2 of 4 videos, the button now says Continue, and it resumes at video 3.
  5. With all four done the assessment unlocks. It is timed at three minutes for the whole attempt.
  6. He passes. The training moves to Completed and the certificate becomes available.

5.4 The admin's flow — publishing

  1. Create the training. Name it, pick the widget view, upload the videos in order — the title fills in from the filename. Add language versions beneath each. Add the questions, or import them.
  2. Place it. Pick the training, the app, the screen, and the position on that screen. Choose the audience. Set the rules.
  3. The console argues back where the configuration cannot work — see §14.
  4. Place it again somewhere else if you want, with different targeting. The training itself is untouched.

6. Where training appears

Nine ways a training can surface. The choice is a judgement about how much of the FSE's attention we are entitled to at that moment.

Mini bannerA persistent, low-cost offer of help on a busy screen. Expands in place; covers nothing.1

Plus My trainings page — not a screen widget at all, but a training that lives only in the hub and is worked through start to finish. Use it for onboarding and anything long.

6.1 Position on the screen

A screen can carry more than one widget of the same type, so choosing a screen also means choosing a named position — “Mini banner — top of page”, “Mini banner — after performance”. The position is what tells two placements apart, and what lets the console warn when two trainings want the same one.

7. The content itself

TypeCounts as finished when
VideoIt plays to the end
AudioIt plays to the end — usable beside a merchant with nothing to look at
PDF or slidesScrolled to the last page
ImageOpened. A single still is consumed at a glance

Languages. Each item has one primary file and any number of language versions beneath it. The player offers exactly what has been uploaded and nothing more — no empty language options. Captions are available on video.

8. Watching

Behaviour
ResumeAlways on, everywhere. Reopening picks up at the furthest point reached, with the position shown and a Start over beside it. No setting can take this away — an FSE interrupted mid-visit must not pay for it twice.
Speed1×, 1.5×, 2×, carried across items for the session. A second viewing is usually a refresher.
Skipping aheadA placement rule, on by default. With it off, seeking is clamped to the furthest point actually watched — scrubbing back and forward within that still works.
Skippable after N viewsAn alternative to the toggle: locked for the first viewings, open afterwards. The player says which view this is and when it opens up.

There is no “I've watched this”. Self-declaring and dragging to the end were the same escape hatch under two names. One control remains, and it is off for anything mandatory — which is the only thing making a mandatory completion record mean anything.

9. Assessment

9.1 Where the questions sit

Decided by the widget, not the author, because not every view has a moment where “the set is finished”.

ViewQuestionsScored
Most viewsAfter the video they belong toPer video
My trainings pageOnce, after all the videosOne paper
StoriesAfter each segment and at the endOne paper across all of it

Why not a combined assessment everywhere. On a carousel the FSE opens the one card relevant to the merchant they are about to see; asking about all twelve afterwards tests content they never opened. On a bottom sheet nothing says they reached the last item. A single pass mark needs a moment to fire on, and only Stories and the hub have one.

9.2 Question types

TypeRule
One answerOne option is correct.
Several answersEvery correct option must be selected and nothing else. Marked whole — there is no part credit. The prompt says how many are correct.
Written answerFree text. Carries no score, sits outside the pass calculation, always asked last. Recorded for the trainer, never marked.

Part credit on a multi-answer question would make a pass mark impossible to reason about, and every question of this kind is one where half-knowing is the failure mode — “which two are required on a rental plan” is not half-right if you name one.

A set containing only written answers is valid. There is nothing to score, so it reports Answers recorded rather than a pass or fail.

9.3 Scoring

Everything is a score. There are no percentages in the model.

  • Each question is worth points — a mis-mapped-QR question can be worth three where a recall question is worth one.
  • The author sets what one paper is worth. Questions are drawn at random until they add up to exactly that.
  • The author sets the score needed to pass, at or below the paper's worth.

Two FSEs sit different papers of equal weight. A retake is drawn fresh too — though because nothing is remembered between attempts it can repeat questions, which §15.5 covers.

Not every total is buildable. Because the target must be met exactly, a pool of questions worth 3, 2 and 1 can make any total from 1 to 6 — but never 7. The console works out which totals are reachable from the uploaded weights, lists them, names the nearest achievable figure, and refuses to save a target that cannot be built. Without that an author could configure a paper the app can never assemble.

9.4 What the FSE sees, and does not

RuleWhy
The correct answer is never shown — during or afterAn answer key turns a retake into a memory test.
No right/wrong feedback per question eitherMarking each selection as it is made is worse than a key: across two or three retakes an FSE can map a whole paper by elimination without watching anything.
Answers can be changed until they move onIt is a test of knowledge, not of tapping accuracy.
Every result reads the sameAssessment passed or Assessment failed, whichever widget it ran in. Three names for one obligation confuses an FSE who meets all three in a week.

9.5 Failing

There is no immediate retry. The content the assessment covers is marked unwatched, so the assessment locks until the FSE has been through it again — a per-video check reopens its own video, a combined assessment reopens the whole set.

This is what makes the pass mark mean anything. With random papers, an instant retry lets an FSE resubmit until a favourable draw appears. Sending them back to the content, with skipping already off on mandatory trainings, makes a second attempt cost what the first did.

9.6 Time limit

One limit per training, covering every question in an attempt. It shows in the header, turns amber at a quarter remaining and red at a tenth. On expiry the attempt is submitted as it stands and graded on what was answered — the result says so rather than showing a bare low score.

9.7 Stories, scored as one paper

Stories is the only view carrying both question types, so its scoring needs stating explicitly.

MomentWhat happens
After a segmentThe answers are scored and carried forward. No verdict is shown — Answers recorded — because a segment is never passed or failed on its own.
Re-answering a segmentReplaces that segment's contribution rather than adding to it, so a segment cannot be farmed for points.
Reaching the end earlyThe closing set is locked until every segment carrying questions has been answered, naming how many are owed. Scoring a partial story would judge it against a smaller pool than the pass mark was set for — making a skipped story easier than a complete one.
At the endThe closing set is added to what was carried, and the total judged once. One result, one message.
On failureEvery segment reopens and the carried total is cleared. The story is retaken whole.

A segment is complete once answered, not once passed — passing is not something a segment can do.

10. My trainings

One list of everything assigned to this FSE.

10.1 The list

  • Four tabs, each with a live count: All, Pending, Pending mandatory, Completed.
  • Order is priority, not upload date: mandatory and part-done first, not-started next, completed last and de-emphasised.
  • Progress counts steps — a step is one video or the assessment. Three videos into a four-step training reads as progress, not zero.
  • A banner names how many mandatory trainings are outstanding, and jumps to that tab.
  • No search. At this list size, priority ordering does the job.

Each card carries the training name, step pips, a status and the MANDATORY tag where it applies. Statuses are: Not started, 2 of 3 videos, Assessment ready, Assessment failed, Assessment passed.

10.2 Inside a training

  • Videos in order. Completed ones ticked; the next one ringed and marked NEXT.
  • A sticky button always naming the next action — Start, Continue, Take the assessment.
  • Where there is a combined assessment it sits at the end, locked until the videos are done, and says why: “Finish the remaining 2 videos to unlock this.”
  • Nothing is locked that the admin has not locked. Videos can be watched out of order unless a rule says otherwise.

10.3 Empty states

Each tab says something useful when empty rather than showing nothing: “Every training assigned to you is done”, “No mandatory work outstanding”, “Finish a training and it will show up here”.

10.4 Certificates

A training can issue one on completion — set per training, so nothing appears the panel has not turned on. It names the FSE and their code, the training, the score where there was an assessment, the date and a reference, and downloads as a file they can keep, share or print. Available from the Completed tab and from inside the training.

11. Mandatory and the homepage blocker

They are one switch, set on the placement: Mandatory training — homepage blocker.

StateWhat the FSE sees
OutstandingThe home screen is sealed. Every blocking training is listed with how many steps remain. The only action is Complete now, which hands off to My trainings. No dismiss, no “later”.
Nothing outstandingNo gate at all.

Why one switch. Content nobody may skip has to be enforced somewhere, and the home screen is the only place we can be sure the FSE passes through. A “mandatory” flag that did not block would be a label, not a rule. Because it lives on the placement, the same training can be mandatory for one audience and merely available to another.

13. Who sees it

An audience comes from exactly one source. They are alternatives, not filters to combine:

SourceFrom
Everyone in the appNo targeting
CleverTap segmentBehavioural segments authored in CleverTap and read over the API — target shortfalls, tenure, portfolio mix
Team / subteamThe reporting structure in the app
Ecode listAn uploaded list of named people

Switching source clears the previous one rather than leaving values that would be saved and never read. A live line states the resolved audience, and it is the same calculation the placements table and the save guard use.

13.1 The CleverTap integration

CleverTap stays the system of record — the console reads segments and never creates one. We store only the segment id, so a segment that changes shape in CleverTap changes who a training reaches with no edit here. That is the point of the integration, and it is why the failure cases have to be loud:

StateBehaviour
Not yet fetchedChoosing the source triggers the fetch. A saved segment id still renders and is not treated as an error before the list arrives.
ReachableSegments listed with their size; footer shows the count and last sync, with a manual refresh.
UnreachableAn error panel with retry. Cached segments are kept and existing placements keep working — only the picker is affected.
Segment withdrawnA placement pointing at an id CleverTap no longer returns is flagged in the editor, flagged in the table, and refused at save. The dead id stays visible so the admin can see what it pointed at.

14. The admin console

Two sections, matching the model: training creation (content, widget, assessment) and placement (app, screen, position, audience, rules).

14.1 What it refuses to save

  • A training with no name, or a video with no title
  • An assessment turned on with no questions
  • A question missing text or any of its options
  • A question with nothing marked correct — it could never be answered right
  • A question weight of zero or less
  • A paper total no combination of weights can reach
  • A passing score above the total, or below 1
  • A placement with no training, screen or position
  • A placement pointing at a CleverTap segment that no longer exists
  • Deleting a training that is still placed — it names where

14.2 What it warns about but allows

  • Two trainings competing for the same position on the same screen
  • The same training placed twice on one screen in different positions
  • A widget view no screen currently carries — it cannot be placed yet
  • A blocker on non-mandatory content, which the FSE gets past by watching
  • Mandatory content not listed in My trainings, which they may never find
  • A skip threshold at or above the frequency cap — skipping would unlock on a view they never reach
  • A skip threshold on mandatory content, where it can never apply
  • A time limit allowing under ten seconds a question
  • A written-answer question with no question text yet

14.3 What it does for you, and says so

  • Turning on a blocker turns on hub listing, because the gate hands off there
  • Reducing a widget's capacity trims the video list and names what it removed
  • Changing widget family discards the assessment shape the other one used
  • Changing a content type clears files that no longer match it
  • A video's title fills in from its filename unless one has been typed

14.4 Importing questions

Questions can be typed or imported as CSV: question, four options, the correct answers, and a weight. Several correct answers are given as 1;3. A written answer is marked by putting text in the correct-answers column, with the hint in the first option. Bad rows are rejected individually and counted, rather than failing the whole file.

15. How questions are chosen

An author uploads a pool of questions. The FSE never sees the whole pool — they see a paper drawn from it. This section is the rule for that draw, stated so it can be implemented and tested without interpretation.

15.1 The three numbers the author sets

NumberSet onMeaning
Question weightEach questionWhat that question is worth. Default 1. A question about mapping to the wrong MID might be worth 3 where a recall question is worth 1.
Paper totalThe question setWhat one attempt is worth. Leave blank to show the whole pool.
Passing scoreThe question setWhat they need to clear it. Must be at or below the paper total.

15.2 The rule

When an FSE opens an assessment, the app builds their paper like this:

  1. Put the written-answer questions to one side. They carry no score and take no part in the draw.
  2. Shuffle the remaining questions into a random order.
  3. Walk that order from the start. Take a question if its weight still fits in the room left; skip it if it does not.
  4. Stop the moment the running total equals the paper total. That is the paper.
  5. If walking the whole shuffled order never lands exactly on the total, discard it, reshuffle and start again. A configuration the console allowed will land within a handful of tries.
  6. Add the written answers back, at the end, in the order they were authored.

15.3 Watching it happen

Take a pool of six questions weighted 3, 3, 2, 2, 1, 1 — twelve points in all. The author has set a paper total of 6.

Suppose the shuffle comes out as 2, 1, 3, 3, 2, 1:

StepQuestionRoom leftDecisionRunning total
1weight 26Fits — take it2
2weight 14Fits — take it3
3weight 33Fits exactly — take it6 — stop

The paper is three questions worth 6. The remaining three are not used this time.

Had the shuffle instead started 3, 3, …, the paper would have been those two questions and stopped at step 2. Had it started 3, 2, 2, …, the third question would have been skipped — no room for a 2 with only 1 left — and the walk would have carried on looking for a 1.

15.4 What the draw is not allowed to depend on

Each paper is drawn fresh, for that FSE, at that moment, from nothing but the pool and the total. The draw keeps no memory and reads no history.

Specifically, it must never consider:

  • What another FSE was shown. We do not track what one person saw in order to decide what a different person gets. There is no shared rotation, no “questions least used across the field force”, no balancing across people.
  • What this FSE saw last time. A retake is drawn the same way as a first attempt.
  • Anything else — time of day, team, tenure, past score.

This keeps the feature honest in two ways. It cannot become a place where one FSE's behaviour quietly shapes another's experience. And it stays testable: given a pool and a total, the set of papers that can come out is fully determined, so QA can enumerate them rather than trying to reproduce a stateful sequence.

15.5 What this means for retakes

Because nothing is remembered, a retake can repeat questions from the failed attempt, and can occasionally be the same paper outright. On the six-question pool above, a retake draws an identical paper about one time in nine, and shares at least one question about nine times in ten.

We are accepting that, for two reasons:

  • Failing already sends the FSE back through the content before they can retry (§9.5). The cost of a second attempt is watching again, not guessing again.
  • Avoiding repeats would mean storing what each person was last shown and consulting it — memory we have just said the draw should not have.

If repeat rate becomes a real complaint, the fix is a larger pool, not a cleverer draw. Twelve questions at a paper total of 6 makes an identical retake vanishingly rare, and that is a content decision an author can make without a code change.

15.6 Which totals are possible

Because the draw must hit the total exactly, not every number can be built. A pool weighted 3, 2 and 1 can make 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 and 6 — but never 7, because no combination of those three adds to it.

The console works this out from the uploaded weights, lists the reachable totals, names the nearest achievable figure when the author misses, and refuses to save an impossible one. Without that an author could publish a paper the app can never assemble, and the FSE would meet a training that cannot start.

15.7 Even coverage

Filling toward a total naturally favours some paper shapes over others — on the pool above, the three-question paper comes up about twice as often as the two- or four-question ones. That is a property of the method, not a problem, provided no question is starved. Across a couple of thousand draws each of the six questions appears between 44% and 59% of the time, so an author who uploads a question can expect it to be asked.

15.8 When no total is set

The whole pool is shown, shuffled, and the paper is worth the pool. This is the right setting for a three-question check after a single video — there is nothing to draw from.

15.9 Stories

Each segment's questions are drawn by the same rule, and each draw's score is carried forward. The closing set is drawn the same way. Only when the last of them is submitted is the carried total judged, once, against the pass mark for the whole story.

16. What we track in the app

Every measure in §3 has to be answerable from what the app sends. These are grouped by the question they exist to answer, not by where they happen to fire.

16.1 Did the nudge land?

EventFires when
Placement shownThe widget rendered on screen for this FSE
OpenedThey tapped it
DismissedThey closed it without opening the content

Shown against opened is the honest measure of whether a placement is help or furniture. Dismissed against shown, per screen and position, is how we decide a nudge is in the wrong place.

16.2 Did they actually watch it?

EventFires when
Play startedPlayback begins
Quarter, half, three-quarter, completeThose points in the timeline are reached
Resumed / restartedThey picked up where they left off, or chose to start over
Speed changedWith the rate
Language switchedWith the language chosen
Skip blockedA forward seek was refused because skipping is off
Content completedPlayed to the end, or the document reached its last page

The quarter marks give the drop-off curve — the single most useful thing for deciding how long a video should be. Language switching tells us which languages to commission next, from behaviour rather than from a survey.

16.3 Did they understand it?

EventFires when
Assessment startedWith the paper's worth, the pass mark and the attempt number
Answer submittedPer question — which question, whether it was right, and how long it took
Segment carried forwardA story segment's score was banked
Passed / failedWith the score and the attempt number
Timed outThe clock expired; how much was answered
Content reopenedA failure sent them back to the videos

Per-question results are what tell us whether a question is doing any work. A question nearly everyone gets right is not testing anything; one nearly everyone gets wrong is usually a badly worded question rather than a badly trained field force, and it should be looked at before the content is.

16.4 Did enforcement work?

EventFires when
Blocker shownThe home screen was sealed, with what was outstanding
Blocker clearedThe last outstanding training was completed
Certificate downloadedThey saved their certificate

Time between blocker shown and blocker cleared is how much working time enforcement costs the field. If that number is large, the content is too long or the gate is too aggressive.

16.5 Carried on every event

FSE and ecode, training, placement, app, screen, position, widget view, audience source, language, and attempt number where one applies. Without placement and position we can compare trainings but not the decisions about where to put them, which is where most of the tuning will happen.

16.6 What we deliberately do not track

  • Written answers do not go to analytics. They are captured for the trainer to read, not to be counted. They are free text an FSE wrote about their own difficulties, and treating them as a metric would change what people write.
  • No location. The app knows where the FSE is for other reasons; training has no business joining that data.
  • No merchant identifiers on training events. A training event says what was watched and by whom, never which merchant was in front of them.

17. Edge cases we have decided

CaseDecision
Assessment with only written answersValid. Reports Answers recorded; no pass mark applies and no content is reopened.

FSE reaches a story's end with segments unansweredThe closing set is locked until they are answered.
FSE re-answers a story segmentReplaces that segment's score.
Training already cleared, rule says do not repeatShows the score they passed with; no re-test.
Language missing for a videoNot offered. The player shows only what was uploaded.
Two mandatory trainings outstandingThe gate lists both, with steps remaining for each.
Merchant interrupts mid-videoPosition is kept; reopening resumes there.

18. What we are not doing

  • No content authoring. Videos are made elsewhere and uploaded.
  • No editing in the app. The FSE consumes; they do not contribute.
  • No new app screens. Training attaches to screens that already exist. The moment we redesign workflows to fit training, we have lost the plot.
  • No offline download in v1, though it is the most likely v2 ask given where FSEs work.
  • No manager-facing reporting screen in v1. Data goes to the existing analytics stack.

20. Appendix — for the build team

Data model, in brief

  • training — name, widget view, time limit, certificate flag, paper total, passing score, result messages
  • training_video — belongs to a training, ordered; content type (video / audio / pdf / image)
  • training_video_file — one row per language; this table alone decides what the player offers
  • placement — training, app, screen, position, audience source and its key, plus the rules in §12
  • assessment_question — belongs to a video or a training; options, correct answers (one or several), weight, or free text
  • progress — per FSE: watched, last position, views, answered, attempts, score, passed, carried total for stories, written answers

Events
