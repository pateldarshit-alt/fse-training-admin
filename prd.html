1. Overview

1.1 Problem

The operating context

A Field Sales Executive works alone. On any given day they are on foot in a market, running a beat of eight to ten merchants, carrying tasks named Onboarding, KYC, EDC Deployment and Soundbox Sale — each with its own SLA, proof requirements and rejection criteria. Their screen shows acceptance windows measured in minutes and breach warnings measured in hours. Nobody is standing next to them.

Every one of those tasks can fail for a knowledge reason: the wrong device type selected, a QR mapped to the wrong MID, a KYC submitted without business proof, a geo-tag captured from the wrong place, a merchant objection that goes unanswered and ends the conversation. These are not motivation failures. They are nobody told me — or nobody told me recently enough failures, and they are addressable.

Four forces make this structural, not incidental

  • Training has a price the FSE personally pays. Time in a video is time not closing a task. An FSE looking at an SLA countdown is being asked to choose between compliance and income. Most choose income, and they are not wrong to.
  • Process changes faster than people can be retrained. Product launches, revised SOPs, pricing changes and new device models reach the field continuously. Each change invalidates what was learned before it, and no mechanism today reliably reaches every affected FSE before the change takes effect.
  • The population turns over. Attrition means the audience for day-one content is never finished. Onboarding is not a project with an end date; it is permanent load.
  • The workforce is not English-first. Content produced in one language reaches a fraction of the people who need it — and the fraction it misses is not random, it skews toward the FSEs who need the coaching most.

Why the current model fails

Training today lives in a section of the app the FSE has no reason to open during a working day. That produces three distinct failures. They are worth separating, because they have different fixes:

  1. Not consumed. Content that has to be sought out, by someone with no time to seek it, is not consumed. This is a distribution problem, not a content problem — better videos in the same place would not move it.
  2. Not retained. Where it is consumed, it is consumed hours or days away from the moment it is needed. A video watched on Monday does not change what an FSE says to a merchant on Thursday.
  3. Not verified. Nothing today distinguishes an FSE who understood the content from one who let it play. For anything the business wants to enforce — a compliance refresher, a mandatory SOP before a launch date — completion is currently a claim, not a fact.

Who bears the cost

WhoHow it shows up
FSEActivities rejected after the work was already done. Incentive lost on effort already spent. A merchant conversation lost to an objection they had no answer for.
MerchantWrong plan explained, wrong device installed, a return visit needed to fix what should have worked first time.

BusinessRework cost on invalid activities, slower time-to-productivity for new hires, and process changes that land unevenly across the field force.

Why now

The distribution problem is solvable without building new app surface area. The workflow screens the FSE already uses — task lists, merchant profiles, device setup, payment collection, performance — are exactly the screens where the knowledge gaps occur, and they can carry training slots additively without changing what those screens do. The targeting signals already exist too: age on network, invalid-activity flags and QC rejection reasons are all captured today. What is missing is the layer that connects them.

1.2 Solution

Deliver short training content at the point of need, inside the workflows the FSE already uses, through a small set of reusable placeholder slots. Pair it with lightweight assessments so knowledge can be verified rather than assumed, and give the business a console to target, sequence and enforce it.

The feature has three parts:

  1. Nine widget views that render training inside existing app screens without altering those screens.
  2. A Trainings & Assessment hub listing everything assigned to the FSE with its status.
  3. An admin console where every behaviour the FSE sees is configured.

1.3 Design principle

The FSE is not a learner. They are a salesperson with a merchant watching and an SLA burning. Training must behave like a tool, not a course. Every design decision in this document resolves in favour of speed and minimal interruption.

1.4 Non-goals

  • This feature does not modify any existing app workflow, screen layout or copy. Widgets are additive slots only.
  • It is not a Learning Management System. There are no courses, certifications or learning paths in v1.
  • It does not author or host video production. Content is uploaded ready-made.
  • It does not replace classroom or field coaching by Team Leads.

2.2 Primary scenarios

S1 — Merchant asks a question mid-pitch

An FSE is on the SoundBox plan screen. The merchant asks what the ₹125 monthly charge covers. The FSE taps an inline card already sitting in the page flow, gets the answer, and continues the conversation. The training was not sought out; it was already where the question arose.

S2 — Day one on the network

A new FSE opens the app for the first time. Mandatory orientation is outstanding, so a gate on the home screen names what is pending and routes them to the hub. They clear it before starting work.

S3 — Repeated invalid activity

An FSE has had activities rejected for the same reason twice. A corrective training is assigned as mandatory. Until it is passed they cannot clear the gate, and their Team Lead can see the outstanding item.

S4 — Process change goes live

A revised SOP takes effect on a fixed date. The training is published as mandatory with the homepage blocker set. Every FSE is gated on their next home screen visit and routed to the hub.

3.2 Common player behaviour

All nine views open the same player, presented in three modes — full-screen, floating, and in-sheet. Behaviour is identical across them.

  • Content types. Video and document (PDF or slide deck) are handled identically. A document renders as a tap-to-open card, opens in a new tab, and counts as consumed on open.
  • Language. The control is always present in the player, so the capability is discoverable. It lists exactly the languages uploaded for that training and nothing else; where only one exists, the sheet says so rather than offering placeholders. Selection persists across trainings, and playback position is preserved across a switch.
  • Captions. A CC control sits beside the language control. Caption tracks are supplied alongside the media file. Where a training has none, the control reports its absence rather than failing silently.
  • Completion. Mandatory content must be watched to the end; optional content may offer a self-declared shortcut if the admin has enabled it. See §5.1.
  • Frequency capping. A training stops surfacing in workflows once it hits its view cap or day cap. It remains findable in the hub either way — a cap governs how hard the platform pushes, never whether the FSE can reach the content.
  • Assessment handoff. Where a training carries an assessment, the call to action is locked until the content is consumed, then becomes the assessment entry point.

3.3 Interstitial rules

The interstitial is the only view that interrupts, so it is the only one with an escape policy.

ConditionBehaviour
Mandatory — assessment mandatory, or homepage blocker setNo skip control is rendered. Clears only when the content is finished and the assessment passed.
Non-mandatoryA Skip for now control is offered. The skip is remembered for the session so the FSE is not interrupted again.
Already clearedNever fires again.
Multiple candidates on one screenOne interstitial only. Priority: homepage blocker, then mandatory, then anything unfinished.

3.4 Homepage mandatory gate

When mandatory trainings are outstanding, the home screen presents a gate. The gate does not play content. Its only job is to name what is pending and hand off to the Trainings & Assessment hub — deliberately moving the interruption off the workflow and into the surface built for it.

TierTriggerBehaviour
Hard gateAny outstanding mandatory training has Homepage blocker enabledNo dismiss control. Primary action routes to the hub. Copy: “These must be completed before you continue.”
Soft gateMandatory trainings outstanding, none flagged as blockerSame sheet plus Remind me later. Shown once per session.
NoneNo mandatory trainings outstandingNo gate.

The gate lists the first three pending trainings with thumbnails and a MANDATORY tag, with a “+N more” affordance beyond that.

4. App — Trainings & Assessment hub

The hub is the single view where an FSE sees everything assigned to them. It is the destination the homepage gate routes to, and the only place training can be browsed rather than encountered.

4.1 Structure

RegionContent
HeaderTitle, “X of Y completed”, and a circular progress ring showing percentage complete.
Alert strip“N mandatory trainings pending”, shown only when the count is above zero.
SearchFree-text search across training titles.
FiltersExactly three: All, Pending, Completed. No other grouping or filtering.
ListA flat, ungrouped list of training cards.

4.2 Training card

Each card carries only what an admin can set. Nothing is displayed that the console cannot control.

  • Thumbnail with duration timestamp and a play badge.
  • Title.
  • MANDATORY tag, shown on mandatory trainings until they are passed.
  • Status: Not started, Take assessment, Retry assessment, or Passed with score.
  • Question count, and a language count where more than one language is uploaded.
  • A left edge accent — red for outstanding mandatory, green for complete.

4.3 Post-completion flow

Passing an assessment inside the hub returns the FSE to the player with a confirmed state and offers the next outstanding training, so a pass leads somewhere rather than dropping them back at the list.

5. Completion and assessment

5.1 Completion rules

Completion is the signal that gates payout-adjacent enforcement, so it must be earned rather than claimed.

ContentHow it can be completed
MandatoryThe video must reach the end, or the document must be opened. No self-declaration is offered under any configuration.
Optional — self-mark enabledReaching the end, or tapping “I’ve watched this video”.
Optional — self-mark disabledReaching the end only.
Mandatory, already passedReaching the end, or the self-mark shortcut. The first clearance was earned; a repeat viewing is a refresher and should not cost the FSE the full runtime again.

Where a training carries an assessment, completion additionally requires a passing score.

5.2 Assessment format

  • Multiple choice, four options per question.
  • Question order is shuffled on every attempt.
  • An optional cap limits how many of the uploaded questions are shown, drawn at random.
  • A configurable pass mark, defaulting to 70%.
  • Unlimited retakes.

5.3 Feedback model

The correct answer is never revealed. Feedback is given per selection, on the FSE’s own choice only.

  • Selecting an option locks it for that question and marks it correct or not correct.
  • Remaining options are disabled and de-emphasised. The right answer is not highlighted.
  • An incorrect selection prompts the FSE to re-watch the content, and notes that the assessment can be retaken.
  • The result screen shows the score and the pass mark only. It does not list questions or answers.

This is deliberate. An end-of-quiz answer key lets an FSE memorise the correct options and clear a retake without engaging with the content, which defeats the purpose of gating anything on the result.

6.2 Live preview

The form renders a phone preview beside it showing how the training will appear in the selected widget view, updating as the configuration changes. This is the primary guard against an admin publishing content into a view it does not suit.

6.3 Library

A table of everything published, showing title with Blocker and Self-mark badges; screen; widget view; every uploaded language; team and ecode targeting; FSE stage; assessment summary; and upload date. The assessment cell expands in place to reveal the full question set with the correct option marked, for review. Rows support edit and delete.

6.4 Validation rules

  • Title and Screen are required.
  • An assessment must have at least one complete question; every question needs text and all four options.
  • Passing score must be between 1 and 100.
  • Max questions to show cannot exceed the number of uploaded questions.
  • Self-marking is force-disabled on save whenever the assessment is mandatory, regardless of the checkbox state.

7.4 Behaviour change — the metrics that justify the feature

These require joining the training stream to activity and payout data. They are the only metrics that answer whether the feature works.

MetricHow it is measured
Rejection rate, before vs. afterInvalid-activity rate for the specific task type a training covers, per FSE, in the 30 days before and after their pass date. The headline number.
Matched-cohort comparisonSame metric against FSEs assigned the training who have not yet completed it, matched on tenure and beat. Controls for the FSEs who would have improved anyway.
Time to first productive activityFor new hires, days from joining to first validated activity, split by whether day-one training was completed in the first 48 hours.
Task completion rate by typeCompletion of the workflow a contextual training sits on, for FSEs who opened it from that screen vs. those who did not.
Repeat-offence rateFor compliance-triggered assignments, whether the same rejection reason recurs within 30 days of passing.
SOP adoption lagDays from a process change going live to 90% of the affected field force passing its training.

The join key is the ecode. Every event must carry it, and the activity and payout systems must expose the same identifier, or none of §7.4 is computable. This is the single most important integration dependency in the project.

9.3 Open questions

  1. Is connectivity in target markets poor enough to make offline caching a launch blocker, or can it follow in Phase 2?
  2. Should mandatory non-completion affect payout directly, or escalate to the Team Lead only? This determines how defensible the completion signal has to be.
  3. What is the acceptable ceiling on interruptions per session before FSEs begin ignoring them?
  4. Which languages are committed for launch, and who owns dubbing and caption production?
  5. Should assessments be deferrable to a downtime moment rather than fired immediately after the video, while the FSE is still in front of a merchant?
