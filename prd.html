1. Overview

1.1 Problem

The operating context

A Field Sales Executive works alone. On any given day they are on foot in a market, running a beat of eight to ten merchants, carrying tasks named Onboarding, KYC, EDC Deployment and Soundbox Sale — each with its own SLA, proof requirements and rejection criteria. Their screen shows acceptance windows measured in minutes and breach warnings measured in hours. Nobody is standing next to them.

Every one of those tasks can fail for a knowledge reason: the wrong device type selected, a QR mapped to the wrong MID, a KYC submitted without business proof, a geo-tag captured from the wrong place, a merchant objection that goes unanswered and ends the conversation. These are not motivation failures. They are nobody told me — or nobody told me recently enough failures, and they are addressable.

Four forces make this structural, not incidental

  • Training has a price the FSE personally pays. Time in a video is time not closing a task. An FSE looking at an SLA countdown is being asked to choose between compliance and income. Most choose income, and they are not wrong to.
  • Process changes faster than people can be retrained. Product launches, revised SOPs, pricing changes and new device models reach the field continuously. Each change invalidates what was learned before it, and no mechanism today reliably reaches every affected FSE before the change takes effect.
  • The population turns over. Attrition means the audience for day-one content is never finished. Onboarding is not a project with an end date; it is permanent load.
  • The workforce is not English-first. Content produced in one language reaches a fraction of the people who need it — and the fraction it misses is not random, it skews toward the FSEs who need the coaching most.

Why the current model fails

Training today lives in a section of the app the FSE has no reason to open during a working day. That produces three distinct failures. They are worth separating, because they have different fixes:

  1. Not consumed. Content that has to be sought out, by someone with no time to seek it, is not consumed. This is a distribution problem, not a content problem — better videos in the same place would not move it.
  2. Not retained. Where it is consumed, it is consumed hours or days away from the moment it is needed. A video watched on Monday does not change what an FSE says to a merchant on Thursday.
  3. Not verified. Nothing today distinguishes an FSE who understood the content from one who let it play. For anything the business wants to enforce — a compliance refresher, a mandatory SOP before a launch date — completion is currently a claim, not a fact.

Who bears the cost

WhoHow it shows up
FSEActivities rejected after the work was already done. Incentive lost on effort already spent. A merchant conversation lost to an objection they had no answer for.
MerchantWrong plan explained, wrong device installed, a return visit needed to fix what should have worked first time.

BusinessRework cost on invalid activities, slower time-to-productivity for new hires, and process changes that land unevenly across the field force.

Why now

The distribution problem is solvable without building new app surface area. The workflow screens the FSE already uses — task lists, merchant profiles, device setup, payment collection, performance — are exactly the screens where the knowledge gaps occur, and they can carry training slots additively without changing what those screens do. The targeting signals already exist too: age on network, invalid-activity flags and QC rejection reasons are all captured today. What is missing is the layer that connects them.

1.2 Solution

Deliver short training content at the point of need, inside the workflows the FSE already uses, through a small set of reusable placeholder slots. Pair it with lightweight assessments so knowledge can be verified rather than assumed, and give the business a console to target, sequence and enforce it.

The feature has three parts:

  1. Nine widget views that render training inside existing app screens without altering those screens.
  2. A My trainings hub listing everything assigned to the FSE with its status.
  3. An admin console where every behaviour the FSE sees is configured.

1.3 Design principle

The FSE is not a learner. They are a salesperson with a merchant watching and an SLA burning. Training must behave like a tool, not a course. Every design decision in this document resolves in favour of speed and minimal interruption.

1.4 Non-goals

  • This feature does not modify any existing app workflow, screen layout or copy. Widgets are additive slots only.
  • It is not a Learning Management System. There are no courses, certifications or learning paths in v1.
  • It does not author or host video production. Content is uploaded ready-made.
  • It does not replace classroom or field coaching by Team Leads.

2.2 Primary scenarios

S1 — Merchant asks a question mid-pitch

An FSE is on the SoundBox plan screen. The merchant asks what the ₹125 monthly charge covers. The FSE taps an inline card already sitting in the page flow, gets the answer, and continues the conversation. The training was not sought out; it was already where the question arose.

S2 — Day one on the network

A new FSE opens the app for the first time. Mandatory orientation is outstanding, so a gate on the home screen names what is pending and routes them to the hub. They clear it before starting work.

S3 — Repeated invalid activity

An FSE has had activities rejected for the same reason twice. A corrective training is assigned as mandatory. Until it is passed they cannot clear the gate, and their Team Lead can see the outstanding item.

S4 — Process change goes live

A revised SOP takes effect on a fixed date. The training is published as mandatory with the homepage blocker set. Every FSE is gated on their next home screen visit and routed to the hub.

3.2 Common player behaviour

All nine views open the same player, presented in three modes — full-screen, floating, and in-sheet. Behaviour is identical across them.

  • Content types. Video and document (PDF or slide deck) are handled identically. A document renders as a tap-to-open card, opens in a new tab, and counts as consumed on open.
  • Language. The control is always present in the player, so the capability is discoverable. It lists exactly the languages uploaded for that training and nothing else; where only one exists, the sheet says so rather than offering placeholders. Selection persists across trainings, and playback position is preserved across a switch.
  • Captions. A CC control sits beside the language control. Caption tracks are supplied alongside the media file. Where a training has none, the control reports its absence rather than failing silently.
  • Completion. Mandatory content must be watched to the end; optional content may offer a self-declared shortcut if the admin has enabled it. See §5.1.
  • Frequency capping. A training stops surfacing in workflows once it hits its view cap or day cap. It remains findable in the hub either way — a cap governs how hard the platform pushes, never whether the FSE can reach the content.
  • Assessment handoff. Where a training carries an assessment, the call to action is locked until the content is consumed, then becomes the assessment entry point.

3.3 Interstitial rules

The interstitial is the only view that interrupts, so it is the only one with an escape policy.

ConditionBehaviour
Mandatory — assessment mandatory, or homepage blocker setNo skip control is rendered. Clears only when the content is finished and the assessment passed.
Non-mandatoryA Skip for now control is offered. The skip is remembered for the session so the FSE is not interrupted again.
Already clearedNever fires again.
Multiple candidates on one screenOne interstitial only. Priority: homepage blocker, then mandatory, then anything unfinished.

3.4 Homepage mandatory gate

When mandatory trainings are outstanding, the home screen presents a gate. The gate does not play content. Its only job is to name what is pending and hand off to the My trainings hub — deliberately moving the interruption off the workflow and into the surface built for it.

TierTriggerBehaviour
Hard gateAny outstanding mandatory training has Homepage blocker enabledNo dismiss control. Primary action routes to the hub. Copy: “These must be completed before you continue.”
Soft gateMandatory trainings outstanding, none flagged as blockerSame sheet plus Remind me later. Shown once per session.
NoneNo mandatory trainings outstandingNo gate.

The gate lists the first three pending trainings with thumbnails and a MANDATORY tag, with a “+N more” affordance beyond that.

4. The content model

Three objects, deliberately separated. Collapsing any two of them is what made the first version of this feature unmanageable.

ObjectWhat it isOwns
VideoA media asset in one or more languages.Its file per language, captions, and optionally its own check questions.
TrainingAn ordered set of videos plus its assessment rules. This is the unit an FSE completes, and it is reusable.Name, widget view, the ordered video list, per-video checks, the combined assessment.

Why the split matters. The same “SoundBox pitch masterclass” can run on GG App Home for everyone and on Onboarding for new Sales FSEs with a 30-day cap — one piece of content, two delivery decisions, one thing to maintain.

5. App — My trainings hub

The hub lists trainings, not videos. It is the destination the homepage gate routes to, and the only place training can be browsed rather than encountered.

A training appears here when its placement has “Also list in My trainings” enabled, or when the placement is the hub.

The hub answers one question before any other: what should I do right now. An FSE opens it between merchant visits, not at a desk, so the design resolves that question above the fold and leaves browsing to the list underneath.

5.1 List view

RegionContent
Header“X of Y complete · N of M steps”, with a percentage ring driven by steps.
Alert strip“N mandatory trainings still to clear”, shown only when above zero. Tapping it switches to the Pending filter.
FiltersFour, each carrying its count: All, Pending, Pending mandatory, Completed. There is no free-text search — at this list size, priority ordering replaces it.
CardsTraining name, step pips, status, assessment size and MANDATORY tag. Nothing else — no source or deadline tags. Completed cards are de-emphasised.

Progress is counted in steps, not trainings

A step is one video, or the combined assessment. Counting whole trainings meant an FSE three videos into a four-video training still read as zero; counting steps makes every action move the number.

Order is priority, not upload date

Cards sort by what deserves attention: mandatory and in progress, then mandatory, then in progress, then not started, then completed. Status is expressed in the unit that matters — Not started, 2 of 3 videos, Assessment ready, Assessment not cleared, Passed · 3/3.

5.2 Training detail — the ordered run

Opening a training shows its videos in order. Each row carries its number, title, language count and the state of its check. The next incomplete step is outlined and tagged NEXT, and a persistent button at the foot of the screen always names that step, so the FSE never has to work out where they were.

The combined assessment is locked until every video is done, and says why — “Finish the remaining 1 video to unlock this.” That lock is what makes it a fair test of the whole set rather than of whichever videos the FSE happened to open.

Individual videos are never locked. The FSE may open any of them in any order. Sequencing is expressed through emphasis — the NEXT tag and the footer button — rather than enforcement, because the admin console has no setting to enforce an order, and the app must not assert rules an administrator cannot see or change.

6. Completion and assessment

Certificates. A training may issue one on completion, set per training in the panel. Completed cards in My trainings then carry a certificate action, and the detail view offers it too. The certificate names the FSE and their code, the training, the score where there was an assessment, the date and a reference, and downloads as a file the FSE can keep, share or print. Nothing appears in the app unless the panel turns it on.

6.1 Two levels

Per-video checkImmediately after each videoIts own question set, pass mark and “show N of M”, edited inside that video’s row.
Combined assessmentAfter every video in the trainingOne question set, one pass mark, one “show N of M”, and the mandatory flag.

The two are independent. Stories typically uses both; a single-video training uses one; a Carousel can only use per-video checks.

6.3 Playback

ControlBehaviour
ResumeAlways on, for every training. Video and audio store the furthest point reached and open there, with the position shown and a Start over beside it. An FSE interrupted mid-visit does not pay for it twice, and there is no setting that can take this away.
Speed1×, 1.5× and 2×, cycled from one control and carried across items for the session. A second viewing is usually a refresher, and 2× respects that.
SkippingA placement rule, on by default and forced off wherever the training is mandatory. With it off, seeking is clamped to the furthest point genuinely reached; scrubbing back and forward within watched content still works.
CaptionsVideo only, toggled from the player.

There is no “I’ve watched this”. Marking a video watched without watching it and dragging to the end were the same escape hatch by two names, so only one control remains — skipping. It is off wherever the training is mandatory, which is the only thing making a mandatory completion record mean anything until completion is measured against the timeline rather than an ended event (§10.1).

6.3a Where an assessment sits

Assessment shape is decided by the widget view, and the two are mutually exclusive.

Widget viewAssessment
My trainings pageOne assessment after all the videos. The FSE opens the training from My trainings, works through the set in order, then takes a single question set covering the whole thing.
StoriesBoth, and scored as one paper. Questions may follow each segment and again at the end; everything answered across the story is added together and judged once.
Every other viewA check sits directly after the video it belongs to. No combined assessment.

A screen widget has no dependable moment at which the set is finished — an FSE opens the one card that matches the merchant in front of them and closes the sheet. A single pass mark across several videos has nothing to fire on, and would test content that was never opened. Putting the check next to the video that taught it is also far easier to author and to reason about.

Mandatory and the homepage blocker are the same thing, expressed once, on the placement: Mandatory training — Homepage blocker. Content nobody may skip has to be enforced somewhere, and the home screen is that place. It follows that the same training can be mandatory for one audience and merely available to another, which is what a placement is for.

6.3b Stories: one paper across every segment

Stories is the one view where both check types coexist, because it is the one view with a dependable running order and a real ending. Its scoring follows from that: there is a single total and a single pass mark for the whole story, not one per segment.

MomentWhat happens
After a segmentThe questions are answered and the score is banked. No verdict is shown — the FSE sees “Answers recorded”, because a single segment is never passed or failed on its own.
Re-answering a segmentReplaces that segment's contribution rather than adding to it, so a segment can never be farmed for points.
At the endThe closing set is added to what was banked, and the total is judged once against the pass mark. One result, one message.
Reaching the end earlyThe closing set is locked until every segment carrying questions has been answered. Scoring a partial story would judge it against a smaller pool than the pass mark was set for, making a skipped story easier than a complete one.
On failureEvery segment reopens and the banked total is cleared. The story is retaken whole.

A segment is complete once it has been answered, not once it has been passed — passing is not a thing a segment can do. The panel reflects this: per-segment rows carry only their questions, and the total score, pass mark and result copy sit once, on the story.

6.4 Question types and weighting

  • Written answer — a free-text response. It carries no weight, sits outside the total score and the pass calculation, and is always asked after the scored questions. Recorded for the trainer, never marked.
  • Single answer — one option is correct; selecting it settles the question.
  • Multiple answers — the FSE must select every correct option and none of the others, then submit. The prompt states how many are correct.
  • Weight — each question carries a weight, defaulting to 1. The score is the weight earned over the weight shown, so a three-point question about mapping to the wrong MID counts for more than a one-point recall question.

A written answer is authored in the panel or imported: in CSV, the word text in the correct-answers column marks the row as free text, and option 1 becomes the hint shown in the box. Because it is unscored, it never appears in the reachable-total maths — a pool of three scored questions and one written answer still reaches the same totals it did without it.

Multi-answer questions are marked whole: a partly correct selection scores nothing. Part credit would make a pass mark impossible to reason about, and every one of these questions is a case where half-knowing is the failure mode.

6.5 Scoring and paper selection

Scores are absolute, in the same unit as question weight. There are no percentages anywhere in the model.

SettingMeaning
Total scoreWhat one paper is worth. Questions are drawn at random until their weights add up to exactly this. Left blank, the whole pool is shown.
Passing scoreThe absolute score needed to clear it. Must be at or below the total.
WeightWhat a single question contributes, and therefore how likely it is to appear in a paper of a given size.

Two FSEs sit different papers worth the same, and a retake draws a fresh one rather than repeating the last. Because the target must be met exactly, not every number is reachable — a pool weighted 3, 2 and 1 can build 1 through 6, but never 7. The console computes the reachable set from the uploaded weights, lists it, names the nearest achievable figure when the target misses, and refuses the save.

6.6 Time limit

One time limit is set on the training and covers every question in an attempt, whichever assessment is running. It appears in the header, turns amber at a quarter remaining and red at a tenth. On expiry the attempt is submitted as it stands and graded on what was answered — the result names the reason rather than showing a bare low score. The console warns when a limit allows under ten seconds a question.

6.6a One vocabulary, every widget

Every assessment reports itself the same way regardless of which widget it ran in: Assessment passed or Assessment failed, followed by the score and the pass mark. A timed attempt that expires reads Assessment failed with the reason underneath rather than a heading of its own.

The surrounding calls to action match: Take the assessment, N-question assessment, Assessment passed. Earlier builds said “quick check” in a mini banner, “check after this segment” in Stories and “combined assessment” on the hub, which read as three different obligations to an FSE who meets all three in a week. They are one thing, and one thing should have one name.

6.6b Sets with nothing to score

A question set may contain only written answers. There is no score, so there is no pass mark to apply: the panel says “nothing here is scored” in place of the scoring fields, and the app reports Answers recorded rather than a verdict. Judging an unscored set against a pass mark produced “Assessment failed · 0 of 0”, which is meaningless, and it also reopened content the FSE had done nothing wrong with.

6.7 Result copy

Pass and fail messages are configured per question set. Defaults are used when left blank. The message is the last thing an FSE reads, so it carries the consequence — a fail on QR mapping says money settles to the wrong account, not better luck next time.

6.8 Feedback model

Nothing is revealed during an attempt. Not the correct answer, and not whether the FSE's own selection was right. The only feedback is the score at the end.

  • Selecting an option highlights it and nothing more. It can be changed freely until the FSE moves on.
  • No question is marked right or wrong mid-attempt, and no option is ever styled as the answer.
  • The result screen shows the score against the pass mark, plus the configured message. No question list, no answer key.

An earlier build marked each selection correct or not as it was made. That was worse than an end-of-quiz answer key: across two or three retakes an FSE could map the whole paper by elimination without ever engaging with the content, which defeats the point of gating anything on the result.

6.8a A failed attempt reopens the content

Failing does not offer an immediate retry. The videos the assessment covers are marked unwatched, so the assessment locks again until the FSE has been through them — a per-video check reopens its own video, a combined assessment reopens the whole set.

This is what makes the pass mark mean something. Retrying straight away lets an FSE brute-force a paper by permutation, especially once questions are drawn at random. Sending them back to the content, with skipping already off wherever the training is mandatory (§6.3), makes a second attempt cost the same as the first.

6.9 The homepage blocker

A placement may mark a training as a homepage blocker. The home screen is then sealed until the training is finished — there is no dismiss and no timer.

StateWhat the FSE sees
Blocker, training outstandingA gate listing every blocking training with how many steps remain. The only action is Complete now, which hands off to My trainings.
Nothing outstandingNo gate at all.

Blocking is a property of the placement, so the same training can gate one audience and merely appear for another.

Homepage blocker is a single switch. The home screen is sealed until the training is finished, with no timer and no dismiss. Turning it on forces the training to be listed in My trainings, since that is where the gate hands off to.

Position on the screen

Choosing a screen then asks for a position — Mini Banner — Top of page, Mini Banner — After performance. A screen can carry more than one widget of the same type, and the position is what tells two placements apart.

Without it, a second Mini Banner on Home is indistinguishable from the first in the data, and the two silently compete for whichever slot the front end renders. With it, the console can say plainly which training already occupies a position and warn that only one of them will show. Two placements on the same screen in different positions are legitimate and pass without complaint.

The slot inventory comes from the front-end build: a generic set per widget type, with screen-specific positions where a screen has somewhere the generic list does not describe. It is a placeholder in this prototype and needs confirming against the real app.

The panel reads segments and never creates or edits one — CleverTap stays the system of record. The contract is four fields per segment: id, name, size and last-updated.

7.3 Rules the console enforces

  • A blocker must be listed in the hub. The gate’s only action is “Go to Trainings”, so a blocker on a training absent from the hub is a dead end. Warned, and corrected on save.
  • Mandatory content should be reachable. Mandatory but not in the hub is flagged — an FSE who never visits that screen could not complete it.
  • A training in use cannot be deleted. Its placements must be removed first, so nothing is orphaned.
  • Duplicate placements are flagged per app and screen, since the two would compete.
  • Question caps cannot exceed the pool, and pass marks must be 1–100.
  • Switching app clears an out-of-app screen rather than leaving an invalid pairing.

The console warns when a view threshold sits at or above the frequency cap — skipping would open on a view the FSE never reaches — and when a threshold is set on mandatory content, where it can never apply.

Added with this revision: a question with nothing marked correct cannot be saved; weights must be positive; changing a content type clears files that no longer match; and warnings fire for a tight time limit, for widely varying weights inside a rotating set, and for a mandatory training that still allows skipping ahead.

8.4 Behaviour change — the metrics that justify the feature

These require joining the training stream to activity and payout data. They are the only metrics that answer whether the feature works.

The join key is the ecode. Every event must carry it, and the activity and payout systems must expose the same identifier, or none of §8.4 is computable. This is the single most important integration dependency in the project.

9.6 Progress

Per FSE, per video and per training: watched, last position in seconds (what resume reads), rotation cursor per question set (which slice the next attempt draws), percent watched, attempted, passed, score, attempts. A training is complete when every video is complete and, where enabled, the combined assessment is passed. Held client-side in the current build — this must move server-side before enforcement is tied to payout or escalation.

10.3 Open questions

  1. Is connectivity in target markets poor enough to make offline caching a launch blocker, or can it follow in Phase 2?
  2. Should mandatory non-completion affect payout directly, or escalate to the Team Lead only? This determines how defensible the completion signal has to be.
  3. What is the acceptable ceiling on interruptions per session before FSEs begin ignoring them?
  4. Which languages are committed for launch, and who owns dubbing and caption production?
  5. Should assessments be deferrable to a downtime moment rather than fired immediately after the video, while the FSE is still in front of a merchant?
