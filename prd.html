Contextual Video Coaching

Training that reaches the FSE inside the job, at the moment it is useful — and proves it landed.

ProductGG App and Force App — Field Sales Executive mobile applications
OwnerProduct
StatusDraft for review

1. Why we are building this

An FSE learns the job in a classroom on day three and is then expected to remember it in front of a merchant six weeks later. Everything since has been a PDF on a group chat.

The cost of that shows up in specific, measurable places:

  • QRs mapped to the wrong MID, so a merchant's money settles into someone else's account
  • SoundBox rentals sold without the UPI Autopay mandate, so the rental never collects
  • Re-KYC left half-finished, so the merchant's settlements freeze
  • Loan pitches made without the disclosures we are obliged to give

These are not knowledge gaps in general. They are four or five specific moments, on four or five specific screens, where the FSE needed thirty seconds of guidance and did not have it.

The bet. Training delivered on the screen where the work happens, at the moment it is needed, in ninety seconds, will change behaviour where classroom training and PDFs have not. And where the content is non-negotiable, we can require proof it was understood.

2. Who this is for

WhoWhat they need
The FSEHelp at the moment of the task, without losing their place. They are standing outside a shop, on a phone, on patchy data, with a merchant waiting.
Their managerTo know who has been trained on what, and to have something to point at in a gate meeting.
L&D and OpsTo publish a training in an afternoon, aim it at the right people, and see whether it worked — without an app release.

3. How we will know it worked

MeasureWhat it tells us
Completion rate of mandatory contentWhether the enforcement mechanism works at all. This is the first thing to watch.
First-attempt pass rateBelow 60%, the content is not teaching what the assessment tests. Above 85%, the questions are too easy to be worth asking.
Error rate on the taught behaviourThe real measure. Mis-mapped QRs, missing Autopay mandates, stalled Re-KYC — before and after, for FSEs who completed versus those who have not.
Dismissal rate per placementWhether a nudge is help or noise. A high dismissal rate on one screen is a placement problem, not a content problem.
Drop-off point within a videoWhere attention is lost. If most FSEs stop at forty seconds, the video should be forty seconds.

4. The shape of the thing

Three objects, and the separation between them is the whole design:

IsAnswers
ContentA video, an audio clip, a PDF or an image — with its language versionsWhat are we showing?
TrainingAn ordered set of content, the widget it appears in, and its assessmentWhat is the FSE meant to learn, and how do we know they did?
PlacementA training put on a screen, for an audience, under rulesWho sees it, where, and how hard does it push?

Why separate training from placement. The same QR-mapping training belongs on the home screen for everyone, and on the merchant screen for FSEs missing their target, with different urgency in each case. One object, placed twice. Without the split, we would be maintaining two copies of the same content and they would drift apart.

6. Assessment

6.1 Where the questions sit

This is decided by the widget, not by the author, because not every view has a moment where "the set is finished".

ViewQuestionsScored
Most viewsAfter the video they belong toPer video
My trainings pageOnce, after all the videosOne paper
StoriesAfter each segment and at the endOne paper across all of it

Why not offer a combined assessment everywhere. On a carousel the FSE opens the one card relevant to the merchant they are about to see. Asking about all twelve afterwards tests content they never opened. On a bottom sheet, nothing says they reached the last item. A single pass mark needs a moment to fire on, and only Stories and the hub have one.

6.2 How scoring works

Everything is expressed as a score, never a percentage.

  • Each question is worth a number of points — a mis-mapped QR question can be worth three where a recall question is worth one.
  • The author sets what one paper is worth. Questions are drawn at random until they add up to exactly that.
  • The author sets the score needed to pass, at or below the paper's worth.

Two FSEs therefore sit different papers of equal weight, and a retake is never the paper just failed.

One consequence to know about. Because the target must be met exactly, not every number is buildable — a pool of questions worth 3, 2 and 1 can make any total from 1 to 6, but never 7. The console works out which totals are reachable, shows them, and refuses a target that cannot be built. Without that check an author could configure a paper the app can never assemble.

6.3 Written answers

A question can ask for a written response instead of options — “What objection did you hear most this week?”. It carries no score, sits outside the pass calculation, and is always asked last. It is recorded for the trainer, never marked. A set containing only written answers is valid and simply reports Answers recorded.

6.4 What the FSE sees, and does not

RuleWhy
The correct answer is never shown — during or afterAn answer key turns a retake into a memory test.
No right/wrong feedback per question eitherMarking each selection as it is made is worse than a key: across two or three retakes an FSE can map the whole paper by elimination without watching anything.
Answers can be changed until they move onIt is a test of knowledge, not of tapping accuracy.
Failing reopens the contentNo instant retry. The videos are marked unwatched and the assessment locks until they have been through them again — otherwise a random paper can simply be brute-forced.
Every result reads the sameAssessment passed or Assessment failed, whichever widget it ran in. Three names for one obligation confuses the FSE meeting all three in a week.

A time limit can be set for the whole attempt. When it expires the attempt is submitted as it stands, graded on what was answered, and says so.

7. Watching

Behaviour
ResumeAlways on, everywhere. An FSE interrupted mid-visit picks up where they stopped. There is no setting that can take this away.
Speed1×, 1.5×, 2×. A second viewing is usually a refresher.
LanguageOnly the languages actually uploaded for that video are offered.
Skipping aheadA placement rule. Off wherever the training is mandatory.

What counts as finishing depends on the content: video and audio play to the end, a PDF is scrolled to the last page, an image is opened.

There is no “I've watched this”. Self-declaring and dragging to the end were the same escape hatch by two names. Only one control remains, and it is off for anything mandatory — which is the only thing making a mandatory completion record mean anything.

8. My trainings

One list of everything assigned to this FSE. Four tabs: All, Pending, Pending mandatory, Completed.

  • Order is priority, not upload date: mandatory and part-done first, completed last.
  • Progress counts steps — three videos into a four-step training reads as progress, not zero.
  • A training can issue a certificate on completion, downloadable from the Completed tab.

9. Rules an admin can set

Set per placement, so the same training can be gentle for one audience and mandatory for another.

RuleEffect
Stop after N views / N daysStops a helpful nudge becoming wallpaper.
Also list in My trainingsFindable in the hub as well as on the screen.
Let the FSE skip aheadOn by default.
Make skippable after N viewsLocked for the first viewings, open afterwards. Replaces the toggle above when set.
Do not repeat the assessment once clearedA repeat visit shows the score they passed with.
Mandatory training — homepage blockerCarries the MANDATORY tag and seals the home screen until it is done.

Mandatory and blocking are one switch, deliberately. Content nobody may skip has to be enforced somewhere, and the home screen is the only place we can be sure the FSE will pass through. A "mandatory" flag that did not block would be a label, not a rule.

10. Who sees it

An audience comes from exactly one source. They are alternatives, not filters to combine:

SourceFrom
Everyone in the appNo targeting
CleverTap segmentBehavioural segments authored in CleverTap and read over the API — target shortfalls, tenure, portfolio mix
Team / subteamThe reporting structure in the app
Ecode listAn uploaded list of named people

CleverTap remains the system of record — the console reads segments, never creates them. We store only the segment id, so a segment that changes shape in CleverTap changes who a training reaches with no edit here. That is the point of the integration, and it is why a segment withdrawn in CleverTap is flagged loudly wherever it was used.

11. The admin console

Two sections, matching the model:

  1. Training creation — content, the widget it appears in, and its assessment.
  2. Placement — app, screen, position on that screen, audience, rules.

Choosing a screen also asks for a position on it — “Mini banner — top of page”, “Mini banner — after performance”. A screen can carry two widgets of the same type, and the position is what tells the placements apart and lets the console warn when two trainings want the same one.

The console will not let an author save something the app cannot honour: a paper that cannot be built, a pass mark above the total, a question with no correct answer, a training placed where its widget does not exist, a blocker that routes to a hub the training is not listed in.

12. What we are not doing

  • No content authoring. Videos are made elsewhere and uploaded.
  • No editing in the app. The FSE consumes; they do not contribute.
  • No new app screens. Training attaches to screens that already exist. This is deliberate — the moment we start redesigning workflows to fit training, we have lost the plot.
  • No offline download in v1, though it is the most likely v2 ask given where FSEs work.

14. Appendix — for the build team

Data model, in brief

  • training — name, widget view, mandatory-by-placement, time limit, allow-seek default, certificate flag, pass score, paper total
  • training_video — belongs to a training, ordered; content type (video / audio / pdf / image)
  • training_video_file — one row per language; this table alone decides what the player offers
  • placement — training, app, screen, position, audience source and its key, plus the rules in §9
  • assessment_question — belongs to a video or to a training; options, correct answers (one or several), weight, or free-text
  • progress — per FSE: watched, last position, answered, attempts, score, passed

Events

Impression, open, play, 25/50/75/100% watched, language switched, dismissed, resumed, restarted, speed changed, seek blocked, assessment started, answer submitted, passed, failed, timed out, content reopened, blocker shown.
